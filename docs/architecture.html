In-memory Ledger: Architecture and Trade-offs

Ankit Kumar · companion to the ledger repository (see DECISIONS.md for the full decision log)

The ledger is a bitemporal, append-only, double-entry store. Every journal carries a value day (which day's balance it belongs to) and a booked day plus global sequence (when the system learned of it). Holds live in a separate append-only authorization log and never touch the money ledger. A single synchronized writer validates each event completely before appending, so every event is all-or-nothing. Current ledger balance and active holds are a write-through cache updated inside the append; value-dated daily closings are folded from the log because back-valued entries rewrite them; a reconciliation pass proves cache equals fold and that every currency nets to zero.

1. Append-only at scale

What breaks first at 100× volume

The end-of-day fee run, not memory. It calls closingBalance once per account per elapsed day, and each call scans the entire, un-partitioned journal list. Cost per end of day is roughly accounts × days elapsed × total journals. With 100× accounts and 100× journals the work per close grows about 10,000×, and it keeps growing every day because the look-back starts at Day 1. The batch overruns its window long before memory is exhausted: fees and interest post late, and the next day's authorizations run against a ledger whose end-of-day state is incomplete. The single writer is not the first bottleneck: an in-memory append is microseconds, and the authorization path already reads O(1) cached balances.

Cheapest structural change that defers it

A per-account, per-value-day net-movement index, maintained write-through. On append, add each posting's amount to bucket[account][valueDay] inside the same atomic step that already updates the balance cache. A closing balance becomes a prefix sum over at most days buckets instead of a scan over all journals; a back-valued entry updates exactly one bucket, so restatement stays correct by construction. The fee run drops from A × D × J to A × D, and reconcile() extends naturally to "index equals fold".

This defers the problem rather than removing it: D still grows. The next step is a back-valuation horizon (see §2): once no entry may be value-dated before day H, everything up to H is sealed into one carried-forward balance per account, the look-back becomes bounded, and sealed journals move to cold storage. That is archiving, not deletion; the log stays append-only. Seen event ids get the same treatment: retained for a window at least as long as the longest upstream retry, then expired.

2. Value-dated entries in production

A value-dated entry changes a balance that other processes have already consumed. In a UAE-licensed bank, each of those consumers is an operational or regulatory exposure.

SurfaceWhat a back-valued entry does to it
Fees and interestRetroactively creates or removes fee triggers and changes interest accrued on past days. This build shows both: fees appear for days that had already closed, and interest is computed on restated balances.
Customer statements and conductBalances on statements already sent no longer match the account. Fees triggered, or left standing, by an entry later reversed are a fair-treatment and fee-transparency issue under the Central Bank of the UAE's consumer protection framework, and a likely complaint.
Decisions already takenAuthorization approvals and declines were made on the balance known at the time and cannot be re-run. A customer can be declined because of a debit that is later reversed.
Accounting period closeAn entry dated into a closed month changes figures already closed in the general ledger and the sub-ledger to GL reconciliation, and potentially financial reporting.
Regulatory returnsReturns to the Central Bank are struck on balances at reporting dates; back-valuing into a reported period means explaining or resubmitting differences.
AML/CFTBack-dating moves the apparent timeline of funds. Transaction monitoring must evaluate on both value time and booking time, and both, plus who approved the back-dating, must be retained for the statutory record-keeping period under the UAE AML law (Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018).
CalendarValue dates must respect the UAE Saturday–Sunday weekend, public holidays, cut-off times, scheme settlement dates and FX value dates for non-AED currencies. The model has integer days only.
Internal fraudBack-valuation is a classic manipulation channel: dating an entry so an account looks funded on a reporting date, earns interest, or avoids a fee.
Control to add before go-live: a back-valuation approval gate. Any entry whose value date is more than one business day before its booking date, or falls in a closed statement or accounting period, is held as pending instead of posted. Posting requires maker-checker approval with a reason code. On approval the system automatically computes and records the customer impact (fees triggered or removed, interest difference) and routes any refund decision; every such entry appears on a daily exception report reviewed by operations and compliance. The same threshold is the horizon that makes §1's sealing possible.

3. Authorization lifecycle

In this model an authorization has three states and only two outcomes that end it: DECLINED at request and SETTLED. Every other way a real authorization ends leaves an APPROVED hold active forever, reducing available balance indefinitely. That is the most important gap to close.

EndingWhat the model does todayReal-world scenarioBehaviour I would mandate
DeclinedDECLINED record with the balances it was decided on; never holds fundsInsufficient funds; also a decline caused by a debit later reversedFinal for that request. If a back-valued correction later shows the decline was caused by bank error, flag it in the customer-impact review; never retro-approve.
Settled for less than the holdSettles, releases the whole holdFinal amount lower: fuel, hotel, restaurantRelease the remainder immediately, unless the authorization is flagged for multi-capture (split shipment), in which case keep it until the final capture or expiry.
Never settledStays APPROVED foreverMerchant never captures: cancelled order, abandoned pre-authorization, depositExpiry by merchant category (shorter for retail, longer for hotel and car rental, per scheme rules): new EXPIRED record, hold released. A later presentment is accepted and flagged as late, not rejected.

Settlement above the holdSettlement refused; hold stays activeTips, incremental chargesTolerance by merchant category. Above it, post the presentment (the issuer is already liable to the scheme), release the hold and raise a dispute case, instead of leaving both a refused settlement and a stale hold.
Settlement refused for another reasonWrong account, already settled, or declined: refused; any hold untouchedDuplicate presentment; mis-routed clearing recordExact duplicates rejected idempotently; everything else goes to a suspense account and exception queue with an SLA, never silently dropped.
Card or account blockedNot modelledLost card, account closureBlock new authorizations; existing holds remain until settled or expired; settlements against them are still honoured.

The mirror case, a settlement with no authorization at all, is refused in this build. In production it is a legitimate force-post or late presentment and belongs in the same suspense-and-review path.
